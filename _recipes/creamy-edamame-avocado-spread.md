A quick, protein-rich plant-based spread that's anything but bland — white miso, lime zest, and toasted sesame oil give it serious depth.

**Flavor variations**
- **Japanese-style:** add 1 tsp rice vinegar + 1 tsp grated ginger, top with furikake or sesame seeds.
- **Middle Eastern:** add 1 tbsp tahini + ½ tsp ground cumin, swap lime for lemon, dust with za'atar.
- **Spicy & bright:** add ½ minced jalapeño + ½ tsp cumin + 2 sliced scallions + ¼ tsp smoked paprika.

### Equipment

- Food processor

### Ingredients

- Edamame (shelled, cooked and cooled) — 250 g
- Ripe avocado — 2 medium
- Garlic — 2–3 cloves, minced
- Fresh lime — 2 tbsp juice + zest of 1 lime
- White miso paste — 1 tbsp
- Fresh cilantro (or basil) — 15 g, chopped
- Olive oil — 1 tbsp
- Toasted sesame oil — 1 tsp
- Sea salt and black pepper — to taste
- Red pepper flakes or sesame seeds — for topping (optional)

### Method

1. Place the shelled, cooked edamame in a food processor and pulse until coarsely ground.
2. Add the avocado flesh, garlic, lime juice and zest, white miso, cilantro, olive oil, and toasted sesame oil.
3. Pulse until the mixture reaches your desired consistency (smooth or slightly chunky).
4. Season with salt and pepper to taste — the miso is already salty, so taste before adding more salt.
5. Serve at room temperature (cold mutes the flavor) or store in the fridge for up to 2 days.

### Storage

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days. The avocado may oxidize slightly on top — press a piece of cling film directly onto the surface to keep it green, and stir before serving.

### Vegan nutritionist comment

This spread is excellent for boosting plant-based protein and healthy monounsaturated fats. Edamame provides the protein and fiber base, avocado adds creaminess, and the white miso brings fermented, gut-friendly depth plus a touch of protein. Miso is naturally salty, so season to taste before adding extra salt. Pair it with whole-grain bread or raw vegetable sticks for a complete, satisfying snack.
